## The canon we position against

**Acemoglu & Restrepo (AER 2018)** — automation as capital displacing labor at
the task margin, with a reinstatement force through new task creation. The
factor "capital" is undifferentiated: it earns its marginal product and its
ownership is outside the model. `refs.bib: acemoglu2018race` (VERIFY).

**Our delta, stated for the paper:** we drop the task margin entirely (the
register's `task_economy` will restore it as the next member) and isolate what
A–R treats as a footnote — the *ownership dynamics of the automating capital
itself*: who holds the stock, what it must pay to exist (upkeep), and where
its earnings go (reinvest / hoard / consume). The knee (P1) has no analogue in
A–R because their capital never has to out-earn a maintenance floor to
survive.

## What the sweep adds (recent/adjacent)

- **Dawid & Neugart 2022** (J. Evol. Econ.) — *the closest methodological
  sibling found*: an agent-based simulation of task automation studying
  industry structure, wage distribution, and the labor share; finds market
  competitiveness/entry barriers condition automation's effects. Use in
  Related Work as evidence the ABM route into task automation is established;
  our difference is the ownership/upkeep focus and the SFC discipline.
- **Charalampidis 2023** (Manchester School) — task-based production inside
  New Keynesian frictions; shows transition dynamics (labor-share falls
  amplified by nominal rigidities). Supports our "prices matter" limitation:
  we pin prices at 1 and say so; this is what a price-bearing successor looks
  like.
- **Knesl 2023** (J. Fin. Econ.-adjacent) — asset-pricing view of
  displacement by capital; peripheral, cite only if the wealth-side framing
  needs an empirical hook.
